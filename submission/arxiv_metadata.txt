Title: Time-Domain Imaging of Rotating, Cloud-Covered Exoplanets with the Solar Gravitational Lens: Reconstruction Algorithms, Observing-Cadence Requirements, and Mission Targets

Authors: Sanskar Sontakke

Primary category: astro-ph.IM
Cross-list: astro-ph.EP
Comments: 24 pages, 8 figures, 13 tables. Revised version: every quoted result re-derived from the shipped seeded pipeline, two explicit resource ledgers for the cadence study, exact anchored slot-offset profiling replacing the approximate deflation operator, and one withdrawn claim (in-flight spin refinement). Simulation code and data archives included as ancillary files.

Abstract:
The solar gravitational lens (SGL) can in principle deliver multipixel images of terrestrial exoplanets from z ~ 650-900 AU. In practice the planet rotates and its clouds evolve while a spacecraft raster-scans the kilometer-scale image cylinder one pixel at a time, so measurements are temporally aliased. We present an open, seeded simulation framework that reproduces the published photometric benchmarks (the aperture-averaged d/4rho kernel; SNR_C = 43.16 per 1800 s dwell on an exo-Earth at 30 pc; the 0.891 D/(d sqrt(N)) deconvolution penalty, matched to within tens of percent and reported as partial validation), and treats rotation, orbital illumination and stochastic advecting clouds explicitly. Recovery is a regularized generalized least-squares inversion of the time-tagged sample stream (time-domain inversion, TDI, not detector time-delay integration) with a block Ornstein-Uhlenbeck cloud covariance. Cloud-free, under known spin geometry, TDI recovers the albedo map at Pearson r = 0.9786 +/- 0.0002 (SSIM 0.848 +/- 0.001, 10 paired seeds): rotational aliasing is tractable under characterized ephemerides, not an information ceiling. Weather dominates the error budget, exceeding coronal shot noise by 163-440x in variance at 1800 s, and fidelity falls to r = 0.249 +/- 0.010 at 71% cover. The cadence law is measured under two mutually exclusive ledgers (8 h of photons per raster position, or 89.87 d of wall clock): 64 short revisits beat 4 long dwells, r = 0.049 -> 0.571 at fixed photons, unanimous across six paired seeds, with the arms agreeing to within the paired standard error; the fiducial campaign is 87.33 d once 45 s per-dwell overheads are charged, and duty cycle falls 99.4% -> 90.9%. Exact anchored profiling of the per-dwell-slot offsets lifts r by +0.054 +/- 0.006 (10/10 seeds, p_t = 1.1e-5) while temporal whitening contributes nothing measurable at the fiducial cadence (-0.003 +/- 0.002, p_t = 0.13). Spin tolerances are measured, not asserted: 5 deg of pole tilt costs 0.057 in r, 5 deg of phase 0.047, and 60 deg destroys the map. We withdraw the earlier claim that misalignments are refinable in flight: the whitened chi^2 is invariant to assumed phase to 1e-11 and monotone in assumed period, so the spin state is observable through image quality only and the ephemeris must come from precursor astrometry. Real spherical-harmonic correlation r(l) exceeds 0.99 up to the l = 20 grid ceiling cloud-free and crosses 0.5 near l ~ 9 (~2200 km) under clouds; land-ocean separation d' = 0.73 +/- 0.03 at 55.8% cover exceeds all 40 members of a cloud-only physical null (p <= 0.024, the ensemble floor). We close with a nominal-proxy target ranking (five confirmed M-dwarf planets plus a refuted tau Cet e row kept for illustration), image-plane dynamics of 1.34-5.81 km/s per 90 d from an explicit integral of projected transverse acceleration, and sailcraft, power and relay discussion; propulsion sizing, site selection and wavelength-integrated photon forecasting are out of scope.
